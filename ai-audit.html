The Monday Morning Audit

Pick one AI use on your team. Not all of them. One. Answer these five questions. It takes about fifteen minutes.

  1. 1. ACCESS

    What can it access?

    Good answer: A written list of what it can read or change, read-only wherever that is enough.

    Warning sign: “Whatever the vendor set up.”

  2. 2. CLAIM

    What is it allowed to claim, and on what evidence?

    Good answer: It says where each fact came from, and what it cannot source is labeled unknown.

    Warning sign: Numbers nobody can trace.

  3. 3. DECIDE

    What does it decide alone?

    Good answer: A short list, every item reversible.

    Warning sign: Nobody can list them.

  4. 4. APPROVE

    Who approves its work?

    Good answer: A named person reviews the full output before it goes out, with risks made visible.

    Warning sign: Approval on a summary, or “the tool handles that.”

  5. 5. OWN

    Whose name is on the outcome?

    Good answer: One name.

    Warning sign: A committee, a vendor, or silence.

If question five does not have one name, that is your governance gap. You found it before it found you.

Before you govern the AI, check the story it is amplifying.

AI scales whichever version of your story it was fed. If your leadership team tells five different versions of why customers choose you, AI will publish all five, faster and more confidently than any person could. That disconnect, between what leadership believes, what your public material says and what the market hears, is the Clarity Gap.

Three questions for your leadership team:

  1. Ask each leader separately: in one sentence, why does a customer choose us over the alternative?
  2. When we lose a deal, who do we lose it to, and why, besides price?
  3. Does our website say what our leaders just said?

If the answers do not match, you have found the gap.
