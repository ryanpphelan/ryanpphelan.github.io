About

After nearly three decades, I’ve learned the answer is usually in the room. Someone just has to help everyone see it.

What I do

People met me through a channel, email, then CRM, then AI, and it was easy to assume the channel was the job. It never was. Behind whatever I was known for at the time, I was running the actual machine: building and leading marketing organizations, owning campaign and client delivery at enterprise scale, setting positioning, and carrying a P&L as CEO of RPE Origin. What ties all of it together, every time, is translation, helping a company and its market finally understand each other. I didn’t arrive at that from the sidelines. I found it by running the operation, not watching it.

A clean lens

When I walk into a company, I’m seldom the smartest person in the room about the product, and I shouldn’t be. Your Head of Product should know the roadmap better than I ever will. What I bring isn’t more expertise. It’s a clean lens. You’re looking at the business through a lens smudged by every meeting, every quarter, every internal debate of the last five years. I’m looking through a clean one. And the fresh eyes I bring are a stand-in for the ones that matter most: your customer’s. My whole job is helping you see your company the way they do. It’s the crooked picture in your own hallway. You walk past it a thousand times and stop seeing it; a guest notices it in three steps. Neither of us is smarter. One of us just has fresh eyes.

Every seat

My career hasn’t followed a straight line, and that turned out to be the point. I started in the dot-com era and have worked every side of this business since: vendor, client, and agency. I studied psychology at a seminary college and learned to code early in my career, which sound unrelated until you notice they teach the same lesson: nothing exists in isolation. You don’t fix the symptom. You trace the system that produced it. Companies work the same way. Marketing isn’t independent of product. Sales isn’t independent of customer success. Every visible problem is fed by a dozen invisible relationships.

The bridge

I’ve come to see executive leaders in three stages: the founder, the bridge, and the closer. The founder brings the vision to life. The bridge takes the company past the ceiling the founder hit. The closer readies it to sell or scale. I’m the bridge. It’s the work I’m built for, upscaling the business, adding clarity, and giving leaders the honest feedback they stop getting once the company is big enough. The best founders know which one they are. The smart ones bring in the others on purpose.

What I believe

  • Strategy before tactics. Always. AI doesn’t change that, it magnifies it. More speed pointed the wrong way is just faster failure.
  • Everybody needs help. Every company has at least one blind spot, no matter how strong the brand.
  • Lift all boats. The job of anyone with experience is to bring people along, not to be the smartest voice in the room.
  • The best advisors make themselves unnecessary. If you still need me to translate your own company back to you a year from now, I haven’t done my job.
