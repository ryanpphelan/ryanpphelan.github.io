The problem

Growth is what breaks the story.

Every company starts clear. Then it succeeds — and success quietly pulls it out of focus.

As you grow, you specialize. Marketing owns demand. Sales owns pipeline. Product owns the roadmap. Customer Success owns retention. Everyone does their job well. No one owns whether it still adds up to one company.

You might recognize a few of these:

  • Ask five leaders what you do, and you get five answers.
  • Sales and marketing describe the company differently.
  • Your website reflects who you were, not who you’ve become.
  • Customers like the product but can’t say what makes you different.
  • Product is innovating faster than the market’s understanding of you.
  • AI is generating activity, not advantage.

These look like separate problems. They’re almost always the same one.

The idea — name it

I call it the Clarity Gap.

The Clarity Gap is what opens when a company can no longer tell one story about itself — when sales, marketing, product, and leadership each describe the business a little differently, and the market feels every inch of the difference.

Every growing company has one. It doesn’t open because leadership got careless — it opens because you got successful, and the business evolved faster than the story you tell about it. The wider it gets, the harder everything gets: sales works harder for the same win, marketing gets busier without moving the business, good products go quiet. Closing that gap is the whole job — and it’s what I do.

The method

I close it with Go-to-Market Alignment.

A structured way to get every customer-facing team reinforcing the same story — instead of six teams telling six.

  1. Market Definition — the market you actually serve, not the one you inherited.
  2. Market Need — the problem your customers are really trying to solve.
  3. Brand Definition — what you should stand for in their minds.
  4. Strategic Positioning — why you win, and why anyone should believe it.
  5. Organizational Alignment — every team reinforcing the same answer.
  6. Market Execution — the story showing up everywhere customers meet you.
